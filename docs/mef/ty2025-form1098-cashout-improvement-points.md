This source route extends the reviewed one-old/one-new cash-out refinance to a new mortgage that pays off old acquisition debt, directly funds a substantial improvement of the same principal residence, and separately pays personal cash-out. This establishes internal source consistency, not independent contractor or bank provenance.

[2025 Publication 936](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936) treats refinancing of acquisition debt as acquisition debt up to the old balance. A new amount used to substantially improve the home is also acquisition debt; the personal portion is not. The mixed-use worksheet applies principal repayments to personal debt first, then acquisition debt. The old unmixed loan retains its months-secured average; the new mixed loan's acquisition and total category closings include zero pre-closing months and divide by 12. The one Table 1 line 14 ratio limits interest and points. Original $250,000 refinance records are untouched; these are separate $300,000 notes with $200,000 payoff, $50,000 contractor payment and $50,000 personal cash-out.

Ordinary points are absent from Form 1098 box 6 and belong on Schedule A line 8c. The new settlement charges $6,000, of which $1,000 is a separately identified service fee and $5,000 is prepaid interest. A reviewed taxpayer savings debit pays all $6,000 at closing; the points were not financed. The improvement share is $5,000 × $50,000/$300,000 = $833.333… deductible in the payment year under Pub. 936's first-six tests, with reviewed local practice/customary charge records and own-funds evidence. The other $4,166.666… is spread over the loan term. The completed Part I points amount is multiplied by the Table 1 decimal before final whole-dollar rounding.
