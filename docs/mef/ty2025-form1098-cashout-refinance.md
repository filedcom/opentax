# TY2025 Form 1098 cash-out refinance source route

The bounded route covers one principal residence, one post-December 15, 2017 acquisition mortgage replaced on the first of a 2025 month by one mortgage, and a positive personal cash-out. It accepts single, joint, head-of-household, and qualifying-surviving-spouse returns; married-filing-separately and multi-property/refinance chains need separate source allocation. It excludes points, Form 8396, other home mortgages, and additional advances.

The two official 2025 Form 1098 Copy B PDFs are retained by exact SHA-256 and checked for printable year, lender, borrower TIN, boxes 1 and 2, and box 3 dates. The reviewed original purchase, new closing, old payoff, dated closing wires, personal-use bank ledger, and monthly lender balances/interest remain referenced workpapers; they are not authenticated lender originals. The original purchase and refinance records must identify the same property; the old payoff wire must identify the payoff receipt; each personal spending row must identify the use ledger. The source model requires every month secured by the old and new mortgage in chronology, reconciles each month's opening balance, payment before closing, closing balance, and interest to its issued Form 1098. The new debt starts at the actual closing disbursements. Principal repayments first reduce the personal cash-out portion under [2025 Pub. 936](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936).

Pub. 936 Table 1 applies different average-balance methods to these two loans. The old loan contains only acquisition debt, so its lender monthly closing balances are divided by the months it secured the home. The new loan contains acquisition and personal debt, so Pub. 936's **mixed-use** method divides each debt-category's January–December balances by 12, including zero balances before its closing. The route sums the old and new averages, caps the qualified average at the post-2017 $750,000 limit, rounds the combined qualified/total ratio to three decimals, and applies that ratio once to both Forms 1098 box 1 interest. The source review reconciles the allocated old and new deductible amounts to Schedule A line 8a; it also sources the line 8 checkbox for proceeds not used to buy, build, or improve the home. Native and direct PDF exporters replay source and filed amount, owner TIN, status, and checkbox/loan-use references. Removing the review from a positive ordinary refinance or altering a closing wire, payoff, use ledger, lender month, 1098, filed amount, or checkbox rejects.

The [official 2025 Pub. 936 PDF, pages 15–16](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p936--2025.pdf) says to add the acquisition and grandfathered category balances for Table 1 and then refer to that figure for line 12. Read literally, that wording omits personal debt, even though line 12 calls for the total average balances of all mortgages and line 13 includes all interest on those mortgages. We use the full mixed-loan principal average on line 12 and the acquisition category average on line 7, so personal cash-out interest cannot become deductible through an omitted denominator. This is an inference reconciling those instructions; the worksheet does not give a complete numerical line 12 example for mixed personal cash-out.

The July packet allocates $5,838 to the old 1098 and $6,568 to the new 1098. Both packets have $200,000 payoff, $250,000 new principal, $50,000 personal cash-out, and $10,000 new-loan principal payments at every month end. New-loan lender interest is paid against each month's opening principal; the Table 1 category averages use closing balances after that month's payment. The old loan is divided by its six or three secured months; the mixed new loan's zero pre-closing months remain in the 12-month denominator.

The initial review packet used the old loan's months-secured denominator for both loans. The [Pub. 936 mixed-use example](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936) instead requires 12 months for the new debt categories. Correcting only that rule changes July's ratio from .960 to .973 and deduction from $12,240 to $12,406 (+$166), and April's ratio from .972 to .976 and deduction from $12,101 to $12,151 (+$50).
